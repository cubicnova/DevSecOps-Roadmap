# Security Risk Assessment Review Checklist

## Scope and context
- [ ] System, business process, scope, and assessment date are recorded.
- [ ] Risk criteria and scoring scale are documented.
- [ ] Business objectives, assets, and affected stakeholders are identified.
- [ ] Assumptions, exclusions, and uncertainty are documented.

## Risk scenario quality
- [ ] Each risk has a unique ID.
- [ ] The scenario describes threat/event, weakness or condition, asset, and consequence.
- [ ] The risk statement is specific enough to support a decision.
- [ ] Relevant threat intelligence, test evidence, architecture, and exposure are considered.
- [ ] Technical and business impacts are distinguished where useful.

## Rating
- [ ] Likelihood has a rationale and timeframe/context.
- [ ] Impact considers confidentiality, integrity, availability, privacy, operations, finances, reputation, and legal obligations as applicable.
- [ ] The approved matrix is used consistently.
- [ ] Scores are not treated as precise probabilities.
- [ ] Mandatory controls or high-consequence scenarios are not ignored due to a low score.

## Controls and treatment
- [ ] Existing controls are implemented and supported by evidence.
- [ ] Planned controls are not misrepresented as current controls.
- [ ] Treatment choice is documented: mitigate, avoid, transfer/share, or accept.
- [ ] Actions are specific, measurable, and testable.
- [ ] Each action has an accountable owner and due date.
- [ ] Dependencies, cost, and operational constraints are considered.

## Residual risk and acceptance
- [ ] Residual likelihood and impact are reassessed after controls are verified.
- [ ] Evidence of implementation and testing is linked or referenced.
- [ ] Residual risk is within the organization's tolerance or escalated.
- [ ] Acceptance includes rationale, scope, compensating controls, and authorized approval.
- [ ] Exceptions have an expiry/review date and conditions for early reassessment.

## Monitoring and maintenance
- [ ] Status and review date are current.
- [ ] Triggers for reassessment are defined.
- [ ] Material architecture changes and incidents prompt a review.
- [ ] Risk owners and stakeholders understand the decision.
- [ ] Closure is supported by verification evidence, not only a status update.
